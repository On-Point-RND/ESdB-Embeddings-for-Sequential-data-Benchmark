Multi-domain sequence representation learning

Towards a Universal Sequence Embedding

A Multi-Domain, Multi-Task Benchmark for Self-Supervised Methods

Can one sequence embedding work across many downstream tasks? We test that assumption on 12 datasets from transactions, recommender logs, time series and text. Seven self-supervised methods and three baselines are evaluated across 42 classification, regression, forecasting and anomaly-detection settings under one protocol.

Core finding

No method is universal. Seven distinct methods win at least one setting, and the strongest method leads in fewer than a quarter of them.

01 · Overview

The choice of embedding is part of the task.

Success on one downstream task transfers only weakly to another. Training loss and label-free quality metrics do not reliably identify a universal model, while per-task selection introduces validator bias. Fusion offers a practical alternative.

Cross-task agreement

Weak alignment is the rule, not the exception.

Off-diagonal relationships are low. Classification and forecasting share essentially no linear variance (R² = 0.00), while even the largest off-diagonal mean rank correlation is only Kendall τ = 0.32.

  • Task performance does not reliably transfer.
  • Method ordering changes with the task family.
  • One aggregate leaderboard hides important trade-offs.

02 · Dataset atlas

Twelve datasets, four structurally different domains.

Each source is converted to the same entity-centric sequence format. Across 42 available settings, 35 targets are constructed from histories, metadata or training statistics and seven compatible source targets are retained.

G

Global targets

One label describes the complete entity history. Entire entities are assigned to disjoint train and test sets.

L

Local targets

Each sampled shift creates a history prefix and a future target window without duplicating the stored events.

42

Unified settings

Task scope is independent of family: classification, regression and anomaly targets may be global or local; forecasting is local.

04 · Ablations

The evaluation protocol changes the conclusion.

Aggregation, downstream validator, hyperparameter budget and checkpoint-selection signal all affect which representation appears best.

A1

Aggregation follows target scope.

The last hidden state is selected in 85% of forecasting and 60% of regression configurations. Mean-pooling is selected in 62% of classification and anomaly settings. Local targets favour recent state; global targets favour whole-history pooling.

A2

Validators introduce measurable bias.

LightGBM has the best average candidate rank, but MLP favours forecasting while the linear probe favours anomaly detection. The same frozen embeddings can be ordered differently by the readout.

A3

Label-free signals do not rescue model selection.

SSL training and validation loss show mixed or even positive correlations with downstream quality. RankMe, LiDAR and aSMI peak at different epochs and do not consistently select the downstream-best checkpoint.

A4

A random encoder is a real baseline.

On some datasets, trained SSL methods add only a small increment above R-RNN or R-Tr; on Favorita regression they all underperform the random RNN. The frontier view separates the contribution of architecture from the contribution of pretraining.

A5

Hyperparameter optimisation is necessary.

Fixed-budget Optuna studies improve downstream metrics substantially—up to 20% in the main analysis, with larger absolute changes in hard settings. Plateau trials vary strongly by dataset, motivating the 70-trial NTP budget.

A6

Multi-target HPO trades trials, not a consistent quality winner.

One 100-trial joint study replaces four task-specific studies on x5-Retail and three on Twitter. Quality moves in both directions; neither regime wins uniformly in this deliberately small comparison.

06 · Evaluation protocol

Comparable splits, frozen embeddings, repeated probes.

Every dataset uses the same entity-centric representation, scope-aware splitting and fixed downstream validators. Probe variation—not repeated SSL pretraining—defines uncertainty intervals.

01

Global Time Split

A shared test boundary is the 0.9 quantile of valid target times. Earlier histories train the encoder and validator; future shifts test them.

02

Global User Split

Complete entity histories are assigned approximately 90/10 to train and test, so downstream evaluation uses unseen entities.

03

Shift construction

Each local shift stores a history prefix plus a horizon-specific future target. Up to 9/1 or 90/10 train/test shifts are sampled per entity.

04

Frozen evaluation

A linear probe is trained with three random seeds. MLP and LightGBM are added on six datasets to quantify validator sensitivity.

Reproducible by construction.

Processed datasets share a Parquet schema, frequency-ranked categorical values, robustly scaled numerical features, saved split metadata and seed-controlled shift sampling. Exact dataset counts and target horizons are available through the cards above.
